Opening llemonAI Wi-Fi Scanner on a Mac

The Mac app isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid Apple developer account), so macOS blocks it the first time you open it. You only have to allow it once:

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag llemonAI Wi-Fi Scanner into the Applications folder. Open it from Applications, not from the disk image.
  2. Double-click the app. macOS says “Apple could not verify ‘llemonAI Wi-Fi Scanner’ is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.” Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to Security. Next to “‘llemonAI Wi-Fi Scanner’ was blocked to protect your Mac”, click Open Anyway.
  4. Enter your Mac password (or use Touch ID), then click Open Anyway once more.

After that it opens normally with a double-click, and updates installed from About → Check for updates open without asking again. The warning doesn't mean anything was found in the app; you can confirm your download is genuine with its SHA256 checksum.

macOS 14 Sonoma or earlier, or using Terminal

On macOS 12–14 you can also right-click (or Control-click) the app in Applications, choose Open, then Open again.

Or, in Terminal, remove the “downloaded from the internet” mark (one time): xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/llemonAI Wi-Fi Scanner.app"

Why these numbers matter

Wi-Fi isn't just signal bars.

Temperature and humidityAffect RF propagation and can stress access point and client hardware in enclosed spaces.
Device load and connected clientsMore clients on a channel means more contention and lower throughput per device.
InterferenceOverlapping channels and non-Wi-Fi RF noise directly reduce usable capacity.
Positioning and obstructionsDistance, walls and line of sight change signal strength far more than most people expect.
Uplink throughputA great Wi-Fi signal can't outrun a slow upstream connection; check both together.
